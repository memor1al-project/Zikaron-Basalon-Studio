Guided interview: three excerpts

Life before the war

“What was their world like before everything changed?”

“My grandfather grew up above his father’s tailor shop, a few streets from where the ghetto wall would later stand. He said his mother sang while she worked, and that the smell of fresh bread from the bakery downstairs was the thing he missed most, for the rest of his life.”

(Noa Rosen, granddaughter)
The war period

“Was there a phrase, a song, or a person that helped them hold on?”

“He rarely spoke about 1943 itself, only about the neighbor who hid him afterward, without being asked twice. He said that one decision, made in seconds, was what he chose to remember. Not the danger.”

(Noa Rosen, granddaughter)
A message for today

“What do you hope the people listening will carry with them?”

“He used to say survival wasn’t the end of the story. It was the beginning of a responsibility. To build something worth having survived for.”

(Noa Rosen, granddaughter)

Historical context, cross-checked

The Warsaw Ghetto was sealed in November 1940, confining several hundred thousand people within a small area of the city.

Mass deportations from the ghetto took place through the summer of 1942, one of the deadliest periods of the occupation.

The Warsaw Ghetto Uprising began in April 1943, among the first large-scale acts of armed Jewish resistance during the Holocaust.
