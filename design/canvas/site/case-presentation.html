Case study: from Yosef’s materials to the Rosens’ evening

With her collection reviewed and approved, Noa opened the Presentation Wizard. Here’s the narrative it drafted, the memorial webpage it built alongside it, and what actually got used on the night.

Narrative draft

Drafted by the wizard, then rewritten in Noa’s own voice.

1
Hook

“Before he became my grandfather, Yosef Rosen was a nineteen-year-old who loved the smell of fresh bread more than almost anything in the world.”

2
Framing

“He was born in 1921 above his father’s tailor shop in Warsaw, the younger of two children, in a home where his mother sang while she worked and Friday nights meant the whole street slowed down.”

3
Heart of the story

“By 1943, the shop, the street and the bakery were gone. What he chose to carry forward wasn’t the danger of those years, but a single act: a neighbor who hid him without hesitation, without being asked twice.”

4
Turning point

“In 1949 he arrived in Tel Aviv with a sewing needle in his pocket and almost nothing else, and within two years he had reopened, in a new language, in a new country, the only trade he had ever known.”

5
Message

“He told us survival wasn’t the end of a story. It was the start of a responsibility. Tonight, we’re passing that responsibility to you.”

Output: the memorial webpage

Yosef Rosen
1921–2009 · Warsaw → Tel Aviv

“We carried what we could, and remembered the rest.”

1921 Born above his father’s tailor shop, Warsaw
1940 Family confined to the Warsaw Ghetto
1943 Hidden by a neighbor after the uprising
1949 Immigrated to Tel Aviv, reopened his trade
2009 Passed away, surrounded by family
With love, the Rosen family · zikaronbasalon.com/stories/yosef-rosen

Output: the evening script

Testimony section · ~45 min

“Before he was my grandfather, he was nineteen, and the thing he remembered most about home wasn’t the danger that came later. It was the smell of fresh bread from the bakery downstairs. Tonight I want to start there, because that’s where he always started too.”

Cue: pass around the tailor’s shears · light a candle for Yosef and Malka

What the room shared afterward

Heart · what did it stir?

“Sad, but also proud. I didn’t expect proud.”

Head · what did you learn?

“I didn’t know ‘hiding’ could mean one decision, made in ten seconds.”

Hands · what could you do?

“Write down my own grandmother’s story before I forget the details.”
