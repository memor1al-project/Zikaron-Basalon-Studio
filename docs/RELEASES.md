# Releases

Generated by `scripts/release.mjs` — edit `app/src/releases/` rather than this document:
`features.json` for what the Studio does, `<version>.json` for what each release changed.

The running version and build number are shown in the app's sidebar; clicking them
opens the same notes in a panel.

## Everything in v0.11.13

### Gathering materials

- Connect a folder on this device, or Google Drive. Folders are read including subfolders, and reattach on later visits without picking them again.
- Add materials from other folders on the device; they reattach too, and can be copied into the story's folder to make them permanent.
- A material that exists both in the folder and as an outside copy is listed once, with its notes kept.
- Tags, notes, a place (searched and shown on a map) and links between materials — 'her father', 'same day as this letter'.
- Materials can be removed from a story and put back. Files on the device are never deleted.

### The catalog

- Everything recorded about a local folder's materials is stored in a SQLite file inside that folder, so it travels with them and survives signing out or changing machine.
- A Catalog page lists every resource with its preview and what has been recorded about it, read from the catalog alone — it still works when the originals can't be reached.
- Previews for photographs, a video's opening frame, and a PDF's first page.
- Coverage figures for each kind of data, the file's size, schema version and history.

### Faces

- Faces are detected on the device and drawn on the photograph itself, not only as a list.
- Selecting a face shows every other material that person appears in.
- A People strip filters the whole collection to one person, grouping the same face across photographs.
- A tagged face can become a person's portrait in the family tree.

### Documents and recordings

- Text is extracted from PDFs and text files in the browser, with no AI service needed. A scanned page with no text layer says so rather than appearing to do nothing.
- Recordings are transcribed with sentence-level timestamps, capped at fifteen minutes so the cost of pressing the button is predictable.
- Summaries, timelines and short narrative passages from any material's text.
- Testimony videos are divided into named chapters with plain-language summaries.

### The family tree

- People, and the families that connect them, stored as GEDCOM family records so a tree imports and exports without losing its shape.
- Add a relative from the person they relate to, or connect two people already in the tree.
- Editing a person lists the families they belong to, so a wrong link can be removed without deleting anyone.
- Links a family record cannot hold are refused with the reason, rather than silently ignored.

### Telling the story

- A guided interview of fourteen questions, reworded from Zikaron BaSalon's own Speakers Kit.
- Answers can be drafted from a document or a transcript — always merged with what a person wrote, never over it, and always saying which material each suggestion came from.
- Moments on a timeline, each able to carry the materials and people it involves.
- Quotes link back to the passage in the source they came from, highlighted in place.

### What a family keeps

- A summary that gathers the subject, the tree, the map, the timeline, the photographs and every answered question into one page.
- A booklet, printed through the browser so the family tree stays sharp as vector.
- An article as Markdown — the family's own words, with the questions kept alongside, and nothing invented to fill gaps.
- A running order for the evening as a deck.

### Example testimonies

- Thirteen real testimonies from Zikaron BaSalon's channel, open to anyone without an account.
- Each divided into chapters, with a navigator built for older viewers: larger text on request, slower playback, chapter-at-a-time controls, captions, and a mode that stops at the end of a chapter.
- Right-to-left throughout, with the navigator's own buttons in the language the survivor speaks.
- Each testimony also worked backwards through the guided interview's questions, with every answer linked to the chapter it came from — and the questions a recording cannot answer left visibly blank.

## v0.7.0 — Backup, and moving a story between computers

*2026-09-22 · first deployed as build 4 on 2026-09-22 16:32*

- A story can be exported as a single archive holding every material and the catalog, so work can be backed up, handed to a family member, or continued on another computer.
- Each archive carries the date it was made, a bill of materials listing every resource in it, and which revision of the catalog it captured.
- A material the catalog remembers but could not reach is still listed in the manifest, marked as missing — an export never looks complete while quietly leaving something out.
- Importing writes the materials back into the connected folder. A file already there is never overwritten, and an existing catalog is never replaced.
- The archive is written as a stream, so a family archive of many gigabytes does not have to fit in memory.
- The Storage step now offers restoring from an export, so a story begun on another computer can be continued without first connecting something else.
- For an archive too large to import in the browser, a small unpacking script can be downloaded for Mac, Linux or Windows — a dialog explains where to put it and what it does before you download it. It needs nothing installed.
